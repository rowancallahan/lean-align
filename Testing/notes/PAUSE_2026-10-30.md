## Measured on chr21, not worth it there yet — re-test both on hg38 (likely different)
Danger regions: hg38 has far more duplicated sequence and repeat-driven cost (mason 2.26M vs 0.35M Ir/pair);
with a per-entry danger bit the check is ~free, so the saving could exceed chr21's 11–12% of CPU. WFA: kernels are
~15–17% of hg38 time (more candidates per read), so a WFA without the old allocation overhead matters more there.
Both need the bench hg38 index (~7.7 GB disk): run on a box with the release.

- **Heuristic (WG_XH=1)** on chr21 sims: +0.8% pairs for +11–14% CPU; its time is chkAln 38%, banded DP 27%, hamD 17%,
  nearY 13%. Gains are mostly mate ties resolved by pairing. Results of `speed/n29-heur`: see below (pending).
- **Danger regions** (`speed/n29-danger`, bench only, default off): on real chr21, 7.0% of the genome is inside an
  exact duplicated 150-mer; ties are 7.7–8.2% of sim pairs and 18–24% of CPU; a "all seeds danger" flag catches
  57–60% of ties at 91–98% precision (11–12% of CPU), but a per-seed check costs more than it saves; a one-bit-per-
  bucket flag catches only 2%. Needs a per-entry bit (index format change). The proved twin (window compare) gives
  identical output but fires on only 2–5% of ties. Script: `Testing/scripts/danger_measure.sh`.
- **Unmapped sims:** ~6.7% genuine repeats (ties); mason ~5% with 5–7 spread mismatches (just over the cap) —
  shorter or spaced seeds would get those; low-error sims have almost none.
- **WFA**: proved kernel exists (not on the router path); a band-pass trial lost its gain to allocation.
